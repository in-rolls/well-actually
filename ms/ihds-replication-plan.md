# IHDS-II independent replication: analysis plan

Date: 2026-09-07. Written before any IHDS outcome-on-dominance model. The author has seen Anderson's results, IHDS documentation, unconditional field ranges, village share coverage and basic purchase/ownership counts. This is a prospective analysis plan for already released observational data, not a registered experiment or an unseen-data prediction exercise. The user authorized execution of the independent replication. Preserve this committed version and record subsequent departures.

## Question and interpretation

Among rural Hindu OBC/SC farming households in Uttar Pradesh and Bihar in 2011–12 with positive owned land, is annual gross crop value per owned acre higher for water buyers in OBC-majority-land villages than in upper-caste-Hindu-majority-land villages? Are village differences larger for buyers without pumps than for pump owners who do not buy water? Report household-weighted conditional associations and intervals. Buyer/owner status, caste geography and land ownership are observational. A causal interpretation would require unobserved agricultural opportunities, settlement, reporting, and selection into buying/owning not to generate these contrasts; this design cannot establish that. No IV is proposed.

The expected Anderson-like sign is positive for the buyer village gap and for buyer-gap minus owner-gap. A 45% higher gross value is a magnitude benchmark, not an exact replication target: gross value differs from cash crop sales, year and sampling geography differ, and the original 45% denominator has not been recovered. Ratios use the weighted observed mean of upper-dominated nonowner buyers in the same estimation sample. Report 10%, 25%, and 45% as scale benchmarks, without declaring 45% causal or uniquely implied by the paper.

## Frozen measurements and population

Read ICPSR36151 DS0002 households, DS0012 villages, DS0014 participant roster from ../land; retain input hashes. Link household-to-village many-to-one on STATEID,DISTID,PSUID, preserving all households and logging misses. R factor numeric codes come from printed labels, never integer factor positions. Participants are many-to-one to village and must first be aggregated; they never multiply household rows.

Primary households: URBAN2011=0; STATEID in9,10; ID11=1; ID13 in3,4; FM1=1; all three owned-area seasons observed and nonnegative; positive FM3; positive maximum(FM4A,FM4B,FM4C)/FM3. The maximum gives annual value per physical owned acre, rather than summing the same acreage over seasons. This is an explicit annual denominator convention, not a physical-yield measure. No landless households are assigned zero yield. Retain exclusions and seasonal disagreements.

Water buyer: observed FM31>0; observed zero is nonbuyer; negative/missing unknown. A zero expenditure does not rule out free water access. Pump owner: any observed positive FM40A/B/C; nonowner only when all three observed zeros; otherwise unknown. Preserve all four buyer/owner combinations in a saturated status interaction; pump ownership does not establish water selling.

Primary outcome: nonnegative observed FM22RSHH / owned_acres, called gross crop value per owned acre. No replacement of missing outcomes. Secondary: INCCROP / owned_acres, retaining negative incomes, and gross value / sum(FM11A:C)/FM3, requiring all three cultivated-area seasons observed and positive sum. The latter is annual value per crop-season acre, not directly comparable in level with owned acres. Log gross value per owned acre on strictly positive values is a transformation sensitivity; zeros and negatives are reported, not shifted by an arbitrary constant. No winsorization in primary fits.

Land shares: for each group, lower=sum VJ6 shares positively identified by VJ3 and Hindu VJ4=1; upper=100 minus shares positively assigned to other groups/religions. Unknown and unlisted shares remain unresolved, with no renormalization. Reject any negative or >100 share or total>100.01. Upper-Hindu group=VJ3 in1,2; OBC-Hindu group=3. At cutoff t, classify OBC only if its lower bound>t and upper-Hindu upper bound<=t; classify upper only if upper lower bound>t and OBC upper bound<=t; otherwise exclude as unresolved/outside contrast. Primary t=50; sensitivity t=40 and60. This compares broad categories, not a verified single-jati majority. IHDS OBC does not isolate Anderson's backward agricultural castes.

Sensitivity definitions: (i) upper majority versus definitely no upper majority at50 (clearly broader estimand); (ii) OBC/SC/ST-Hindu majority versus upper majority at50; (iii) strongest single reported Hindu OBC jati >50 versus strongest single upper-Hindu jati >50, with shares not rescaled. Unknown membership cannot imply a majority. Continuous-share and largest-group models are deferred because incomplete lists do not identify exact shares/ranks; no midpoint is substituted for the unknown land.

## Estimation, inference and reporting

Primary weighted least squares: outcome ~ dominance * four-level water status + household size + household maximum education + SC indicator + district fixed effects (state-district key). Controls are descriptive composition adjustments, not asserted pre-treatment causes. WT is the household survey weight; do not treat as inverse-variance weight. Primary contrast1 is the OBC-minus-upper gap for nonowner buyers; contrast2 is that gap minus the gap for nonbuying owners. Retain both/neither contrasts as secondary. Use village CR2, explicitly inverse_var=FALSE, and Satterthwaite contrast-specific degrees of freedom. Report sample households, villages by dominance/status, coefficient identification and district overlap. If a contrast is not identified, retain NA and its reason rather than arbitrary omitted-coefficient normalization.

Fixed sensitivity list: state rather than district effects; equal household weights; equal total village weights; cutoffs40/60; broader dominance definitions above; OBC-only and SC-only households; separate UP and Bihar; exclude owned holdings<0.1acre; 99th percentile upper winsorization of outcome within the eligible two-state sample pooled across dominance; secondary outcomes above. Common-sample comparisons for alternate outcomes where feasible. Every run is reported with its changed sample; no specification search stops at a desired answer. Other Indian states are a separate deferred geographic extension, not pooled into the primary result.

Primary inference family comprises buyer gap and buyer-minus-owner difference. Report marginal95% intervals and Holm-adjusted p-values for these two; no claim from isolated secondary p-values. Sensitivities are estimates and intervals, not independent confirmations. Show HC1 and village CR1 alongside CR2 for the primary model. If either dominance group has<40 villages, add 999-draw null-imposed wild village bootstrap checks for the two primary contrasts using fixed seed20260907. No randomization inference: no known randomized assignment.

For the two primary gaps divided by the observed upper-village nonowner-buyer mean, resample whole villages within state999 times (seed20260908), refit the same model, recompute its same-draw denominator, and report paired percentile intervals plus failed/undefined draws. These are descriptive relative-scale intervals and may differ from CR2 absolute-scale intervals. Report no ratio if its reference mean is nonpositive.

Before seeing estimates, report group/status sample counts and weight concentration. After fitting, leave each village out once for the primary buyer gap and report the whole range with sign reversals, including both supportive and weakening results. This is influence analysis, not a probability distribution of classification errors.

## Measurement checks and potential rival explanations

Count participants per IHDS village and tabulate respondent caste and position, missing roster coverage, and whether OBC/SC and upper-Hindu participants are represented. Do not infer independent agreement from participant counts: there is one collective village response. No respondent caste/leadership variable is a valid instrument. Report correlations of exposure with village reported upper population share, household SC share, household size/education, owned acreage, landlessness among all target caste households, and water status as composition/selection diagnostics. These are not untreated placebos for longstanding caste institutions. True pre-period geography/census linkage is unavailable; mark that test unrun.

Self-audit transformations, unit conversions, missingness, linkage, reference cells and estimability before models; obtain an independent design/code reader. Test bounds and unknown-share classification with hand-calculated fixtures, seasonal conversion and missing pump status; verify estimates by an independent WLS calculation and contrast identities. Generate report values from saved outputs. Do not redistribute local survey household data in the public repository.

## Pre-estimation audit decisions

An independent reader reviewed the plan and local instruments before outcomes were fitted. The resolvable-majority restriction defines a selected sample; the main claim is limited to those villages. Only two SC-majority villages are available. All comparisons with fewer than five villages in either exposure group will retain an insufficient-support record rather than an inferential estimate. Five is a reporting floor, not a claim of adequate precision. Primary support is743 households/87 villages (28 upper/59 OBC), with only7 upper-village nonbuying-owner clusters. There are36 districts,16 with both exposure categories. Report these diagnostics and CR2 degrees of freedom. The numerical audit finds13 overlapping districts with exactly one upper village, correcting the reader’s count of12.

The village questionnaire lists jatis selected by population size. A single-jati majority means a majority for a reported population-listed jati; it does not reveal the ranking of all landowners. The user guide (printed p.23) lists FM22RSHH as crop income separately from residue and expenses, and the questionnaire records crop production and prices. We analyze this released crop-value aggregate, without claiming to have reproduced its undistributed crop-level construction. No cash-sales or physical-yield interpretation is allowed.

The household WT and village CR2 analysis is a weighted conditional association, not a full reconstruction of stratified survey inference: strata, finite-population corrections and panel attrition/refreshment are not modeled. Selection, missingness, district overlap, weight concentration and respondent representation are saved before estimation. All four water statuses remain in the model; water spending conflates need, quantity, price and purchase, and ownership is not verified selling. Sensitivity conclusions must be conditional on this limited support.
